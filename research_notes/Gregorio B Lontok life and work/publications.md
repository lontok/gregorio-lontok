# Publications and scholarly mentions of Gregorio B. Lontok

Method note for the report writer. Sources checked were Internet Archive OCR text of about 300 digitized issues of The Philippine Agriculturist (1911-1965) and the Philippine Journal of Agriculture (1930-1950), all grepped for "Lontok". I also ran Google Books full-text search, both across the whole corpus and inside individual digitized volumes of Sugar News, PSA reports, USDA inventories, and the Bibliography of Agriculture. Consensus and web search covered the scientific context. HathiTrust full-text search blocked automated access. The Internet Archive full-text search API returned HTTP 502, and The Sugar News is not on Internet Archive. So HathiTrust and archive.org full-text search remain unchecked.

Status codes used below: (a) independently confirmed, (b) partially confirmed, and (c) only in the family document.

Google Books links point to the digitized volume with a search for "Lontok". Page numbers came from Google's search-inside results. Several Sugar News volumes are full view, so a reader can open the page and read the article.

## Which listed items can be independently confirmed?

### Takeaway
Eight of the nineteen visible items are confirmed or partly confirmed in digitized sources. Every one of them falls in the sugar years (1926-1928) or the 1950s Agricultural and Industrial Life period. The family list gets some Sugar News volume numbers and page numbers wrong. The 1927 articles are in Vol. VIII, not Vol. VII, and the mosaic article is in Vol. VIII (1927) at pp. 997-998, not Vol. IX pp. 56-58. The spot-checked page numbers are otherwise accurate.

### Inferences
- The family list reads like a CV he compiled himself. Where it can be checked, the titles and page numbers mostly hold up, and the errors are volume numbers and one reused page range. That pattern points to typing mistakes, not invented items, which raises confidence in the unverified circulars.
- The Spanish circulars of 1930-31 (items 9, 11, 12, and 14) match results he published or co-harvested in Sugar News in 1929-30. They were probably Spanish-language extension notices to La Carlota planters that restated those results.

### Gaps
- The Spanish edition of the 1926 PSA committee reports was not located, so the pp. 17-19 citation is unverified.
- The 1927 Sugar News p. 926 item could not be read. Search-inside returns only three hits per query, so there may be more Lontok items in Vol. VIII.
- Philippines Free Press 1924, Agricultural Life 1930, and 1953 Agricultural and Industrial Life, the Sunday Herald, and the Philippines Herald are not digitized in any source I could search.
- The rest of the family list (beyond item 19) was not supplied, so it could not be checked.

## Scientific context: P.O.J. 2878, Badila, mosaic, and phosphate

### Takeaway
His 1926-1931 work sat at the center of the main problem in world cane growing at the time. Mosaic virus was wrecking the old "noble" canes. Java's P.O.J. 2878 was the new resistant hybrid, and Philippine planters were testing it, together with Badila and New Guinea canes, against the local Negros Purple. His fertilizer trials asked whether Negros soils needed phosphorus and potash on top of the nitrogen planters already used.

### Cited findings
- Sugarcane breeding in Java began at the Proefstation Oost-Java (POJ) in the late nineteenth century. Its "great achievements were the discovery of 'wonder cane' POJ 2878 and POJ 3016". Source: [Widyasari et al. 2021, Sugar Tech](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4544fb2cf4fd52f6b4c6725d62e53d3d/?utm_source=claude_code).
- Mosaic disease "was first noticed in Java in 1892 and is now a very serious problem in all sugarcane areas except in Mauritius," a 1927 description. Source: [Madras Agricultural Journal 1927](https://consensus.app/papers/details/cd5e07a5b58a5899b6b1acd20a8b9886/?utm_source=claude_code).
- In Puerto Rico a 1916-19 mosaic epidemic put the industry "on the verge of destruction." The "introduction and propagation of mosaic-resistant varieties" and "rogueing" of infected stools saved it. Source: [Garcia et al. 1957, Journal of Agriculture of the University of Puerto Rico](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d6d5f85c0f1b5fb4bd6cda0abf30d33e/?utm_source=claude_code). Mosaic was brought under control there after "the importation ... and planting on large scale of mosaic-resistant P.O.J. sugarcane varieties". Source: [González Ríos and Adsuar](https://consensus.app/papers/details/1d91e20844ac56cca80fa9cfcbf1d06c/?utm_source=claude_code). Noble varieties were later "replaced by P.O.J. 2878". Source: [Liu et al., Journal of Agriculture of the University of Puerto Rico](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c6a276c388e95aea8f1400d0ba9d974d/?utm_source=claude_code).
- In the United States, a breeding program "was established in 1919 to overcome a devastating mosaic virus epidemic". Source: [Hale et al. 2021, Sugar Tech](https://consensus.app/papers/details/65f2de57e76f551e934d023572c3c8f3/?utm_source=claude_code).
- In the Philippines, Galloway's survey of Southeast Asian sugar notes that "the overall best-performing variety was Badila, a naturally occurring variety of Saccharum officinarum." It also refers to a 1930-31 cane census in which P.O.J. 2878 figured. I saw only this search excerpt, not the full paywalled text. Source: [Galloway 2005, Geographical Review, JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/30034307).
- His own trials show the same shift locally. P.O.J. 2878 outyielded all other varieties, and Negros Purple "has in every case been outyielded". ### Inferences
- For family readers, the story runs in a line. In the late 1920s, a virus disease was damaging cane in the Philippines, as it had in other cane countries. He was one of the Filipino field men who tested the Java replacement cane farm by farm around La Carlota, and he helped show planters it beat their old variety. His Spanish circulars of 1930-31 likely carried those results to planters.
- His fertilizer work tested whether adding phosphorus and potash (P and K) to the usual nitrogen paid on Negros soils. The title of item 11, which names a "best source of phosphoric acid," suggests he reached a practical recommendation. That remains unverified.
