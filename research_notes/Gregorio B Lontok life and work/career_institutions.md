## 1. Does Gregorio B. Lontok appear in the Official Gazette, BPI reports, civil service records, Sugar News, or newspapers?

### Takeaway
Yes, extensively in period sugar and agricultural serials, which independently confirm most of the family record's career outline: 1920s experiment work at Binalbagan, La Carlota, and Ma-ao under the Philippine Sugar Association; a Sara-Ajuy post in the early 1930s; La Granja in 1949; and Superintendent of La Granja Experiment Station, Bureau of Plant Industry, by 1957. I found nothing on officialgazette.gov.ph, and no source for the 1948 dual status, the Feb 15, 1957 appointment date, the income figures, or the "doubled yield" claim.

## 2. History of La Granja Experiment Station

### Takeaway
La Granja began as the Spanish "Granja Modelo" at La Carlota. Sources give 1881 (decree), 1884-85 (first trials), 1887, or "about 1890" as founding dates. The Bureau of Agriculture revived it in the early American period as the La Carlota (Sugar-Cane) Experiment Station, and it later became the BPI La Granja (Sugarcane) Experiment Station. Sugarcane was always its main work, with vegetables, fruits, and other crops added later. In 1976 it became part of the La Granja Agricultural Research Center consortium. Its sugar arm is today's SRA La Granja Agricultural Research and Extension Center (LGAREC).

### Cited Findings
- La Carlota city government: a Royal Decree of November 15, 1881 created the "La Granja AGRICOLAS [sic]." The decree was promulgated on July 8, 1884, and La Granja opened in Barrio Cubay, La Carlota ([La Carlota City, History](https://lacarlotacity.gov.ph/history)).
- An 1896 Boletín Oficial Agrícola de Filipinas article on "La Granja Modelo de La Carlota" refers to "1884-85" and first trials in the second semester ([Boletín Oficial Agrícola de Filipinas, 1896](https://books.google.com/books?id=6dsXAQAAIAAJ)).
- Filomeno Aguilar cites Spanish archival files on the "Granja-modelo de Visayas (La Carlota), 1887" (AHN Ultramar, Legajo 473) ([Aguilar, Clash of Spirits, 1998](https://books.google.com/books?id=Z58BEAAAQBAJ)).
- The 1908 Revista agrícola de Filipinas says the farm "was established by the Spanish government around 1890 and was long under the charge of agronomist engineer D. José Sánchez" ([Revista agrícola de Filipinas, 1908](https://books.google.com/books?id=g1MjAQAAIAAJ)). A 1929 Bureau of Agriculture paper says the same and calls Sánchez a soil man ([Miscellaneous Papers on Sugar Cane and Fertilizers, 1929](https://books.google.com/books?id=SYxCAQAAIAAJ)).
- Sugar News (1930): "The La Carlota Sugar-Cane Experiment Station was organized by Don Jose Sanchez about 1890, when it was known as the La Granja Modelo. During the Filipino independent government the station was managed by General Juan Araneta ..." ([Sugar News, 1930](https://books.google.com/books?id=gZgrAQAAIAAJ)).
- Conflict: the city site says 1881 decree and 1884 opening. Early 20th-century sources say about 1890. A 1941 bibliography cites a report on La Granja Modelo "en el año de 1893." The dates probably mark different stages (decree, siting, full operation), but the sources do not resolve this.
- Philippine Agricultural Review (1923): "La Carlota Experiment Station was established during the Spanish regime and it was known as La Granja Modelo. The station was established to meet the demand of the planters in Negros ..." ([Philippine Agricultural Review, 1923](https://books.google.com/books?id=wPLNAAAAMAAJ)).
- Librero (1988): operations were disrupted by the Filipino-American War in 1899, and the station "was also revived in 1903 under the management of the then Bureau of Agriculture" ([Librero, Estimating Returns to Research Investment in Sugarcane, 1988](https://books.google.com/books?id=YRxmsz9xid0C)).
- The 1928 PSA Annual Reports say "La Granja Modelo, or as it is known officially by the Government, the La Carlota Sugar-Cane Experiment Station of the Bureau of Agriculture, is located nine kilometers to the east of the municipality of La Carlota" ([PSA Annual Reports, 1928](https://books.google.com/books?id=IA0iAQAAIAAJ)).
- By 1947 to 1950 it was the BPI "La Granja Sugarcane Experiment Station," which hybridized and selected the Alunan cane and also did "research work ... in connection with vegetables and fruits" ([Report of the Secretary of Agriculture and Natural Resources, 1950](https://books.google.com/books?id=QPdMAAAAYAAJ); [USDA Inventory of Seeds and Plants Imported, 1950](https://books.google.com/books?id=7BObGPkpJ54C)). A 1952 BPI retrospective says a cane from the station "matures in 12 months and yields about 120 ..." ([A Half-century of Philippine Agriculture, 1952](https://books.google.com/books?id=zRmI782xUn4C)). Felix D. Maramba published "The La Granja Experiment Station of the Bureau of Plant Industry" around 1949 ([Biological Abstracts, 1950](https://books.google.com/books?id=d8Axt4ZkMdgC)).
- Carlos Quirino (1974): the Bureau of Agriculture (later BPI) turned La Granja Modelo into an experiment station. La Granja "assisted the PSA researchers through the services of technical personnel and the use of their laboratories" ([Quirino, History of the Philippine Sugar Industry, 1974](https://books.google.com/books?id=7tMzAAAAMAAJ)). A 1972 PHILSUGIN Quarterly also notes the station was "owned by the Bureau of Plant Industry" ([Philippine Sugar Institute Quarterly, 1972](https://books.google.com/books?id=Y1AaAAAAIAAJ)).
- UP College of Agriculture branch at La Granja: RA 2415 (June 21, 1959), with the UPLB La Granja Research and Training Station established March 1, 1970 ([UP OVPPA document](https://publicservice.up.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/SFP_OJT.pdf); [Corpus Juris RA list](https://thecorpusjuris.com/legislative/republic-acts/)).
- The La Granja Agricultural Research Center (LGARC) was established in 1976 "to bring together the research units of the Philippine Sugar Commission (PHILSUCOM), BAI and BPI that were already located at La Granja," and UPLB joined later ([Agricultural Research System of the Philippines, AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/8431/files/edc81-03.pdf); [PCARR review, 1980](https://books.google.com/books?id=ePbwWhbrqiwC)). Current members: SRA-LGAREC, BPI La Granja National Crop Research, Development and Production Support Center, DA-NOROS, PCC La Carlota Stock Farm, and UPLB-LGRTS ([UP OVPPA document](https://publicservice.up.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/SFP_OJT.pdf)). SRA-LGAREC focuses on high-yield cane varieties and farmer training ([UPV Cultural Mapping](https://cmpg.upv.edu.ph/heritage/sugar-regulatory-administration-la-granja-agricultural-research-and-extension-center-sra-lgarec)).

- Lontok's 1928 to 1931 PSA experiments in La Carlota district ran alongside the government station, which Quirino says lent PSA researchers staff and labs. His later move to La Granja was a return to an institution he knew from the 1920s.

## 3. History of La Carlota, Binalbagan, and Ma-ao centrals and their experiment departments in the 1920s

### Takeaway
All three were large "modern" centrifugal centrals built in the 1916 to 1921 boom. Ma-ao and Binalbagan were financed by the Philippine National Bank. La Carlota was an Ynchausti/Elizalde enterprise. In the 1920s the Philippine Sugar Association ran a centralized experiment program under G. H. Pritchett and Manuel L. Roxas, with PSA assistants posted at each mill district. Lontok was one of these.

### Cited Findings
- La Carlota: Central Azucarera de La Carlota was founded in 1916 by the Elizalde and Ynchausti families at Hacienda Esperanza (company-history reposts). It absorbed the Sta. Teresita and Kanlaon mills in 1920, began operating in crop year 1920-21, and expanded to three mills in 1925 ([CACI "About" page](https://lacarlotasugarcentral.wordpress.com/about); [Old Negros Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/oldnegros/posts/9608244265876031)). The city site gives 1918 as the founding year ([La Carlota City, History](https://lacarlotacity.gov.ph/history)). Conflict: 1916 vs 1918.
- Wikipedia's Ynchausti article lists La Carlota among Ynchausti y Compañía's sugar assets and says the firm divested most holdings by 1936, with Elizalde & Co. as successor ([Wikipedia, Ynchausti y Compañía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynchausti_y_Compa%C3%B1%C3%ADa)). Lead only.
- Ma-ao: a PNB-financed central built 1919-20 with P6,350,000 in original financing. Production went from 265,127 piculs (1922-23) to 934,008 piculs (1933-34) ([Larkin, Sugar and the Origins of Modern Philippine Society, Table 13](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/sugar-and-the-origins-of-modern-philippine-society.html)). Founded by Jorge L. Araneta in Bago, with operations starting in 1920 ([Old Negros Facebook group](https://www.facebook.com/groups/oldnegros/posts/2750664048300788)). Lead only.
- Binalbagan: a PNB-financed central built 1920-21 with P5,000,000 in original financing. Production went from 231,532 piculs (1922-23) to 1,032,866 (1933-34). Originally founded by the Yulo family, it changed hands in 1919 to American Philip Whitaker, financed almost wholly by PNB credit, amid corruption involving PNB president Venancio Concepcion ([Larkin, UC Press](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/sugar-and-the-origins-of-modern-philippine-society.html)). It was later merged into the Binalbagan-Isabela Sugar Co. (BISCOM), which appears by name in the 1957 PHILSUTECH list ([PHILSUTECH 1957](https://books.google.com/books?id=sdRNAAAAYAAJ)).
- PSA experiment organization: the 1926 Handbook and conference proceedings list the "Experiment Staff: G. H. Pritchett, Director of Experimental Work; Dr. Manuel L. Roxas, In Charge of ..." with field assistants at Bacolod-Murcia, Calamba, Ma-ao, Isabela, Binalbagan, Pampanga, and Hawaiian-Philippine ([Handbook, 1926](https://books.google.com/books?id=VdNwMRy16ikC); [PSA Proceedings, 1926](https://books.google.com/books?id=PZ0VAQAAIAAJ)). In 1928 H. Atherton Lee planned La Carlota fertilizer experiments that Lontok harvested ([Sugar News, 1929](https://books.google.com/books?id=fZcrAQAAIAAJ)). By 1928 the PSA listed district men for La Carlota, Isabela, Binalbagan, Sara-Ajuy, San Carlos, Bais, Cebu, and Mindoro ([PSA Research Bureau Reports, 1928](https://books.google.com/books?id=wDZPAAAAYAAJ)).

### Inferences
- The "experiment departments" of these centrals in the 1920s were largely PSA cooperative programs run through the PSA Research Bureau, with joint experiments involving the central, a planter's hacienda, and the PSA. Lontok's documented experiments follow that model exactly.

### Gaps
- Internal central-level experiment departments (separate from the PSA) were not documented.
- The founding date of La Carlota differs by source (1916 vs 1918).

## 4. The sugar industry in Sara and Ajuy, Iloilo, in the 1930s

### Takeaway
Sara-Ajuy's modern sugar economy centered on the Central Azucarera de Sara-Ajuy, incorporated by Ynchausti y Cía around 1928 to 1929. Output rose quickly in 1929 to 1931, and the mill became Iloilo's top producer by the late 1930s. Lontok's 1933-34 roles fit this growth phase.

### Cited Findings
- The Central Azucarera de Sara-Ajuy was incorporated in 1928 by Ynchausti y Cía, with a mill in Ajuy. Production was 1,842 tons (1929), 4,046 (1930), and 4,716 (1931). It was Iloilo's top producer by the late 1930s and produced 108,725.52 piculs in 1941-42. It halted in the war and closed after a brief postwar restart ([Wikipedia, Sara, Iloilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara,_Iloilo), which cites the Governor General's 1929 report, the American Chamber of Commerce Journal of Aug 1931, and Funtecha 1997). Lead only; the underlying sources were not checked.
- Planters formed the Sara-Ajuy Sugar Planters' Association, led by Victorino Salcedo, who became an assemblyman in 1935 ([NISU Sara Campus history](https://www.nisu.edu.ph/index.php/external-campuses/sara-campus)).
- PSA Research Bureau variety trials in 1931 included "Sara-Ajuy Mill-site" plots planted Nov 28, 1931 ([PSA Research Bureau Annual Report, 1931](https://books.google.com/books?id=KKQVAQAAIAAJ)). Sara-Ajuy had been a PSA district since at least 1928 ([PSA Reports, 1928](https://books.google.com/books?id=wDZPAAAAYAAJ)).
- The 1933 Hacienda Serruco first-year crop was 11,110 piculs at 96 piculs/ha under administrator Lontok ([Sugar News, 1933](https://books.google.com/books?id=CpsrAQAAIAAJ)). In 1934 Gregorio B. Lontok was Technical Adviser, Hacienda Ynchausti & Co., Sara-Ajuy ([Philippine Agriculturist, 1934](https://books.google.com/books?id=9bsctQIbjZsC)).

### Inferences
- The family claim of hacienda demonstrations in "central Sara-Ajuy" fits his documented role as technical adviser to the Ynchausti haciendas supplying the Ynchausti-owned central. Wikipedia's figures show mill output rising in these years, but the jump predates his arrival (1929 to 1931), so the doubling claim cannot be attributed to him from these sources.

### Gaps
- District-level yield per hectare for Sara-Ajuy for 1931 to 1937 was not found, so "doubled the district's yield" cannot be tested.
- It is unclear whether Hacienda Serruco belonged to Ynchausti & Co.
- One Facebook repost says the Ynchaustis sold the central to the Elizaldes in 1923, which conflicts with the 1928 incorporation date. It is unverified, and I did not use it.
