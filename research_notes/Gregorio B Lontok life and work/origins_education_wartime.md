# Gregorio B. Lontok: origins, education, and wartime experience

Labels used below. (a) means independently confirmed by a source outside the family document. (b) means partially confirmed or consistent with documented context. (c) means found only in the family document so far. Spellings vary in the records as Lontok, Lontoc, and Lontac. The OCR of the 1920 program even reads "Grogoiio B. Lontac." Two other College of Agriculture men named Lontok appear in the same years. Ambrosio M. Lontok (Lobo, Batangas, B.Agr. 1920) and Mariano Lontok (a student in 1918) are different people and should not be merged with Gregorio.

## Is there a record of Gregorio Lontok at the UP College of Agriculture, and what did B.Agr. and B.S.A. mean at the time?

### Takeaway
Yes. College and University publications from 1917 to 1934 confirm that Gregorio B. Lontok studied at Los Baños, graduated Bachelor of Agriculture on April 5, 1920, and is listed with the B.S.A. (Supplementary) class of 1923. At the time, B.Agr. was the six-year course for intermediate-school graduates. The B.S.A. (Supplementary) was a two-year add-on that "raised" the B.Agr. to a B.S.A. so the holder could become a master's candidate. No record of M.S.A. work turned up.

### Cited Findings
- A USDA fungus specimen (BPI 770534, Schizophyllum commune) lists "Lontok, Gregorio" as collector at Los Baños, Philippines, in December 1917. This puts him at the College as a student by late 1917. (a) Source: [USDA ARS Biocollections, BPI record list](https://biocollections.ars.usda.gov/collections/list.php?taxa=159212&usethes=1&taxontype=2&comingFrom=newsearch&page=5)
- The College student roster printed in November 1918 lists "Lontok, Ambrosio," "Lontok, Gregorio," and "Lontok, Mariano" as students. (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist and Forester, vol. 7, no. 4, Nov. 1918](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1918-11_7_4)
- A December 1919 review of College rice research by Nemesio B. Mendiola says that "a piece of work has been started, under the writer, by Gregorio Lontoc" to find out which supposedly permanent external characters of rice varieties actually vary. The study used five varieties. This was his senior research under Mendiola in the Division of Plant Breeding, Department of Agronomy. (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 8, no. 5, Dec. 1919, "A review of the rice investigations at the College of Agriculture"](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1919-12_8_5)
- The College and Alumni Notes (p. 263) say that "the following seniors were graduated on April 5, 1920." "Lontoc, Gregorio" is no. 16 under Bachelors of Agriculture, and "Lontoc, Ambrosio" is no. 15. (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist and Forester, vol. 8, no. 7, 1920](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1920-02_8_7). The volume index lists "Lontoc, Gregorio, cited, 157, notes on, 263." Source: [vol. 8 index](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1920_8_index)
- The University's 1920 commencement program lists "Gregorio B. Lontac, Rosario, Batangas" among the 1920 Bachelors of Agriculture. His hometown is given as Rosario, not Taysan. (a) Source: [UP Commencement exercises, 1920, full text](https://archive.org/stream/acg5458.1920.001.umich.edu/acg5458.1920.001.umich.edu_djvu.txt)
- The 1922 commencement program lists "Lontoc, Gregorio B." under 1920 Bachelors of Agriculture. Its alumni directory gives "Lontok, Gregorio B. (B.Agr. '20), Rosario, Batangas; College of Agriculture, Los Baños, Laguna." (a) Source: [UP Commencement exercises, 1922, full text](https://archive.org/download/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu_djvu.txt)
- The College's directory of all its graduates, "The Output of the College of Agriculture," was printed in vol. XI in 1922-23. Under the Class of 1920 it lists "Lontok, Gregorio B., B.S.A. (Univ. Phil.), In Charge, Citrus Project, United States Department of Agriculture, Los Baños, Laguna." Under the Class of 1923 (OCR reads "GLASS OF 1923"), in a list headed "Bachelor of Science in Agriculture (Supplementary)," it lists "Lontok, Gregorio B. See Class of 1920, Bachelor of Agriculture, supra." (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. XI (1922-23), full text](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.229390/2015.229390.The-Philippine_djvu.txt)
- A June 1923 alumni address list gives "Lontok, Gregorio B., '20, Los Baños, Laguna." (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 12, no. 1, June 1923](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1923-06_12_1)
- A June 1924 thesis abstract is signed "Abstract by Gregorio B. Lontok," with the footnote "Abstract prepared as part of required theme work in English 101, College of Agriculture." It summarizes R. A. Reyes's thesis on water supply and rice yields. (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 13, no. 1, June 1924](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1924-06_13_1). The 1927 cumulative index credits the same abstract to him. Source: [vol. 15, no. 8, Jan. 1927](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1927-01_15_8)
- The Silver Jubilee alumni roster (vol. XXIII, June 1934) lists "Gregorio B. Lontok, Technical Adviser, Hacienda Ynchausti & Co., Sara-Ajuy, Iloilo" under "Private Agricultural and Other Enterprises: Farm Managers." In 1934 he was working on Panay, not Negros. (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 23, 1934-35](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1934-1935_23)
- In 1919 and 1920 the College advertised two undergraduate tracks. The first was "a six-year course (the first two years' work being preparatory only) based on graduation with good grades from the intermediate schools... leading to the degree of Bachelor of Agriculture." The second was "a four-year course based on the completion of the public high school course, and leading to the degree of Bachelor of Science in Agriculture." (a) Source: [The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 8, no. 5, Dec. 1919, College advertisement](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1919-12_8_5). Earlier 1916 wording is the same apart from the preparatory note. Source: [vol. 5, no. 4, Aug. 1916](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1916-08_5_4)
- College registrar Evett D. Hester explained the supplementary B.S.A. in 1924. Some U.S. universities had accepted B.Agr. graduates "with his acknowledged shortage of two years' work in preparatory literature and history" as master's candidates. But "when the University of the Philippines established its own Master's system it ruled against the Bachelor of Agriculture graduates." The fix was a supplementary curriculum of history, English literature and composition, and academic electives. It "normally takes two years and, upon satisfactory completion, the degree of Bachelor of Agriculture is raised to the degree of Bachelor of Science in Agriculture, thus removing the barrier to candidacy." (a) Source: [Hester, "Relation of the College of Agriculture to lower schools," The Philippine Agriculturist, vol. 12, no. 10, Mar. 1924](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1924-03_12_10)
- Several of his 1920 B.Agr. classmates show the same pattern of B.Agr. first and B.S.A. later. Examples are "Capinpin, Jose M. (B.Agr. '20, B.S.A. '22)" and "Carandang, Atanacio T. (B.Agr. '20, B.S.A. ...)". (a) Source: [UP Commencement exercises, 1922](https://archive.org/download/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu_djvu.txt)
- For comparison, Ambrosio M. Lontoc of Lobo wrote a 1920 B.Agr. thesis on corn. He later appears as a Batangas farm adviser (1922-23) and as a Bureau of Agricultural Extension district agriculturist (1953). He is a different person. Sources: [PA vol. 10, no. 6, Jan. 1922](https://archive.org/details/philippine-agricultural-scientist_1922-01_10_6), [PA vol. XI directory](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.229390/2015.229390.The-Philippine_djvu.txt), and [PA vol. 36 (1952-53)](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.267994/2015.267994.The-Philippine_djvu.txt)
- Claim check. "Alumnus of the UP College of Agriculture at Los Baños" is (a). "Holds B.Agr." is (a), with the degree dated April 5, 1920. "Holds B.S.A." is (a), listed with the supplementary B.S.A. class of 1923 and styled "B.S.A. (Univ. Phil.)" in the College directory. "Finished all M.S.A. requirements except the thesis" is (c). The supplementary B.S.A. existed to open master's candidacy, so the claim is plausible, but no M.S.A. record was found.

### Inferences
- The six-year B.Agr. track and the Taysan birth fit together. He likely entered from an intermediate school around 1914, which matches the 1917 specimen and the 1918 roster. This is an inference and no enrollment date was found.
- The English 101 abstract (1924) fits the supplementary curriculum's English literature and composition requirement. The directory lists him with the 1923 supplementary class, so he may have finished that coursework around 1923-24. The exact conferral date needs a 1923 or 1924 commencement program.
- He was "In Charge, Citrus Project, USDA, Los Baños" around 1922-23 and a hacienda technical adviser in Iloilo in 1934. That career path could explain how he later ended up in Negros sugar country. Nothing found documents when he moved to Negros Occidental.

### Gaps
- The 1923 and 1924 UP commencement programs are not on archive.org. Only the 1917, 1919, 1920, 1921, and 1922 programs are online. They would confirm the B.S.A. conferral date.
- No record of M.S.A. enrollment or a master's thesis was found in 275 downloaded issues of The Philippine Agriculturist (1915-1960). The OCR is imperfect, so a missed mention is possible.
- The title and date of his own 1920 B.Agr. thesis on rice variability were not found. The 1919 review marks the work with a footnote reference, but the reference list was not recovered from the OCR.
- In the 1921 commencement alumni list, only Ambrosio's name was found in the OCR. The list seems to break at a page boundary. Source: [UP 1921 commencement PDF](https://kahimyang.com/resources/books/UP-11th-commencement.pdf)
- HathiTrust full-text search could not be queried from this environment.

## Taysan, Batangas around the 1890s-1910s, and whether Lontok is a Batangas surname

### Takeaway
The family says he was born in Taysan, but his college records list his home as Rosario, Batangas. The two are not in conflict. From 1903 until a 1918 executive order, Taysan was legally part of the municipality of Rosario, so it had no separate existence through his school years. Lontok is a documented southern Batangas surname in this period, with Lontoks from Lobo, Rosario, and Lipa.

### Cited Findings
- Taysan traces its origin to Barrio Mercedes of Rosario. It was a separate town in Sastrón's 1895 account, and "Taysan was officially made an independent municipality in 1919." (a) Source: [Wikipedia, Taysan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taysan)
- The Rosario municipal history quotes Act No. 958 of 23 October 1903, which reduced Batangas's municipalities: "The Municipality of Rosario shall consist of the present territory and that of the Municipality of Taysan." It adds that Taysan "was again made a separate municipality" by Governor-General Harrison's Executive Order No. 43 of 11 November 1918. (a) Source: [LGU Rosario, Batangas, History](https://www.lgurosariobatangas.com/p/history.html)
- Rosario was where Gen. Miguel Malvar made peace with Gen. J. Franklin Bell on 16 April 1902, ending the Philippine-American War in Batangas. His early childhood therefore overlapped with the revolution and the Philippine-American War in this district. (b, context) Source: [Wikipedia, Rosario, Batangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario,_Batangas)
- The 1920 and 1922 UP programs give his residence as "Rosario, Batangas." (a) Source: [UP 1922 commencement](https://archive.org/download/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu/acg5458.1922.001.umich.edu_djvu.txt)
- The U.S. Social Security NUMIDENT file, as transcribed by a genealogy aggregator, gives his birthplace as "BATANGAS BAT." (b, province only) Source: [SortedByName, Lontok](https://sortedbyname.com/letter_l/lontok.html)
- Lontoks in Batangas at the time include Ambrosio Lontok y Mendoza of Lobo (B.Agr. 1920) and Gregorio B. Lontac of Rosario. Source: [UP 1920 commencement](https://archive.org/stream/acg5458.1920.001.umich.edu/acg5458.1920.001.umich.edu_djvu.txt). The Find a Grave index also lists a Felix R. Lontok (1894-1938) and a Soledad Lontok (1897-1978) buried in Lipa, plus other Lontoks in Batangas. Source: [Ancestry, Find a Grave global index search for "lontok"](https://www.ancestry.co.uk/search/collections/60541?name=_lontok)
- Surname websites describe Lontok and Lontoc as Tagalog Philippine surnames. One site says the name has early concentrations in Batangas. These are low-authority aggregators. (b) Sources: [Forebears, Lontok](https://forebears.io/surnames/lontok) and [names.org, Lontoc](https://www.names.org/n/lontoc/about)
- Claim check. "Born in Taysan, Batangas" is (b). No birth or baptismal record was found. Batangas birthplace is (a), and Rosario residence is (a). Taysan lay inside Rosario's legal territory from 1903 to 1918, which makes the family claim consistent with the records.

### Inferences
- If he was born in December 1894 (see the genealogy section), Taysan was still a Spanish-era town. From 1903 to 1918 it was a barrio of Rosario. A college clerk in 1920 would have recorded "Rosario" as his hometown even for someone born in Taysan.

### Gaps
- No baptismal or civil birth record was found. Catholic parish registers for Taysan or Rosario (FamilySearch "Philippines, Catholic Church Records") would be the next place to check. FamilySearch could not be searched without logging in.
- No scholarly source on Taysan life in the 1890s-1910s was retrieved beyond municipal and encyclopedia summaries.

## Negros Occidental under Japanese occupation (1942-1945): food shortages, root crops, the named towns, and Hacienda Corazon

### Takeaway
The general picture in the family account is well documented. Evacuation from the northern Negros sugar towns, food scarcity, inflation, and guerrilla and civilian food-production drives all appear in the sources. No independent source was found for a named "famine" in 1943-44 across Cadiz, Manapla, Victorias, Saravia, and Silay. Nothing documents Gregorio Lontok's 16 hectares of cassava either. A present-day "Hda. Corazon" does exist in Victorias City, but no wartime record of it was found.

### Cited Findings
- The Japanese first landed on Negros on 21 May 1942. "Countless Negrenses were frantically rushing to find evacuation places in the upper mountainous areas." In the sugar areas "the urgent food supplies problem was not solved because prices had become high, hoarding was spreading and the Japanese gave priority to their own needs." (b, context) Source: [Hofileña, "Negros Occidental under Japanese occupation (Part 2)," SunStar Bacolod, 13 July 2021](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/bacolod/opinion/hofilea-negros-occidental-under-japanese-occupation-part-2)
- The Free Negros civil government under Gov. Alfredo Montelibano Sr. was set up on 16 December 1942. In the hills "lack of food was prevalent," and "the resistance government also established areas for palay and other crops." (b, context) Source: [Hofileña, Part 3, SunStar Bacolod, 20 July 2021](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/bacolod/opinion/hofilea-negros-occidental-under-japanese-occupation-part-3)
- A peer-reviewed study of Negros sugar planter Jose Gaston of Hda. Sta. Rosalia, Manapla, rests on his wartime papers. It shows that civilians in the Manapla and Victorias area had palay, livestock, and equipment taken by guerrillas and by the Japanese. One loss in early 1943 was "120 cavans [1 cavan = approx. 44 kg.] of palay." The Manapla Sub-Sector fell under Maj. Ernesto Mata's North Negros Sector, which covered "Silay-Victorias... and Cadiz-Manapla." (b, context for the exact towns named) Source: [Josefina Dalupan Hofileña, "Life in the Occupied Zone: One Negros Planter's Experience of War," Journal of Southeast Asian Studies, March 1996](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-southeast-asian-studies/article/abs/life-in-the-occupied-zone-one-negros-planters-experience-of-war/281D5394585397F311844C41F8FFEF29)
- A Victorias local history says the Japanese entered Victorias on 27 May 1942. Residents evacuated "to the mountains and neighboring haciendas," food "was rationed," and American forces arrived on 26 April 1945. This is a local blog, not a scholarly source. (b) Source: [A Pinoy in Korea, "Chapter 17: Victorias life during World War II"](https://apinoyinkorea.blogspot.com/2019/11/philippine-history-chapter-17-victorias.html)
- Bulado's study of Negros Oriental, the other half of the island, draws on the R. B. Silliman World War II Papers. It analyzes how the Japanese occupation, the guerrilla struggle, and civilian compromises shaped food supply, and it emphasizes "the vital role of food in the wartime survival" of all groups. Its references include 1944 municipal "food production supervisor" records and an "extensive food production campaign" in Tanjay (Aug. 1944). (b, island-wide context) Sources: [Bulado, Philippine Studies 2023, via Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7a53390539b65c01b4d4ee41e58f460a/?utm_source=claude_code) and [Academia.edu page with reference list](https://www.academia.edu/129045287/_We_All_Must_Work_Fight_or_Starve_The_Food_Supply_Problem_in_Negros_Oriental_during_the_Japanese_Occupation)
- Nationally, the Japanese Military Administration and the Laurel government tried to raise food output and ration rice, and "almost all the plans failed." Manila had an actual rice shortage by November 1943. (b, context) Source: [Ricardo T. Jose, "The problem of food and inflation," NAST Transactions 20 (1998)](http://www.nast.dost.gov.ph/images/pdf%20files/Publications/NAST%20Transactions/NAST%201998%20Transactions%20Volume%2020/SSD%204%20%20The%20Problem%20of%20Food%20and%20Inflation,A%20Case%20Study%20from%20the%20Japanese%20Occupation-Ricardo%20T.%20Jose%201998.pdf)
- A parallel Visayan case, Leyte in 1942-44, shows food procurement contested among the Japanese, guerrillas, collaborators, and residents. (b, comparative) Source: [Ara, "Food supply problem in Leyte... (1942-44)," JSEAS 2008, via Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fffeec9cc41751a3a51e288afdd99623/?utm_source=claude_code)
- Cassava is widely described as a "drought, war and famine crop" because it yields on poor soils with few inputs. The same review warns that its cyanogenic glycosides require proper processing. (b, general agronomic context) Source: [Burns et al., "Cassava: the drought, war and famine crop in a changing world," Sustainability 2010, via Consensus](https://consensus.app/papers/details/efc735b2a745543fbf7aadf2a1e43ee0/?utm_source=claude_code)
- By the end of the occupation a kilogram of camote (sweet potato) reportedly cost about 1,000 "Mickey Mouse" pesos. (b, national context, secondary website) Source: [HistClo, Philippines WWII food](https://histclo.com/essay/war/ww2/cou/phl/eco/pe-food.html)
- Place check. Manapla is about 8.5 km northeast of Victorias. Cadiz, Silay, and Magalona (Saravia) all lie within about 25 km. Source: [PhilAtlas, Manapla](https://www.philatlas.com/visayas/r06/negros-occidental/manapla.html). Saravia is the former name of today's Enrique B. Magalona. Source: [PhilAtlas, Enrique B. Magalona](https://www.philatlas.com/visayas/r06/negros-occidental/enrique-b-magalona.html)
- Hacienda Corazon. A Facebook page for the "HDA. Corazon Youth Organization" gives its location as Barangay X, Victorias City. A Victorias buy-and-sell group post gives a location as "Brgy Estado Hda corazon." This shows a present-day Hda. Corazon in Victorias. No wartime or pre-war record of it was found. (b, existence only) Sources: [Facebook, HDA. Corazon Youth Organization](https://www.facebook.com/61576689123612/photos/122166324938889637/) and [Facebook, Victorias Ugyon Baligyaan post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/281534989510016/posts/1817159825947517/)
- Claim check. "Lived in Manapla during WWII" is (c). "Children attended Manapla Elementary School" is (c). "Evacuated to Hacienda Corazon, Victorias" is (b) for the hacienda's present-day existence and (c) for his stay there. "Planted cassava on 16 hectares" is (c). "Famine struck Negros Island in 1943-1944" is (b). Severe food scarcity is documented, but no source found uses "famine" for northern Negros Occidental in those years. "People from five towns lived for three months on his cassava" is (c).

### Inferences
- Evacuees from Manapla and Victorias commonly moved to "neighboring haciendas" and upland areas, and both guerrillas and the Japanese requisitioned rice. A trained agronomist planting a fast, low-input root crop on hacienda land fits the documented conditions. The scale claimed (16 ha feeding people from five towns for three months) is not something any source found can test.
- The North Negros Sector grouped Silay-Victorias and Cadiz-Manapla under one guerrilla command. That may be why the family account names this exact cluster of towns.

### Gaps
- No source found names Gregorio Lontok in wartime Negros. The Jose Gaston Papers (Hda. Sta. Rosalia, Manapla), the Negros Occidental Historical Commission oral histories, and the 1953 Historical Data Papers for Manapla and Victorias barrios at the National Library are the likeliest places to find him or Hda. Corazon.
- The full text of Josefina Hofileña's 1996 article and of Bulado's 2023 article could not be read, only the abstracts and reference lists.
- No record of a prewar or wartime Manapla Elementary School enrollment was found. School records were not searched, and details on living relatives were intentionally not collected.
- Guerrilla or Free Government food-production records for northern Negros Occidental (for example in PVAO guerrilla files) were not located online.

## Genealogical records for Gregorio B. Lontok

### Takeaway
Two independent indexes agree that Gregorio B. Lontok was born on 24 December 1894 in Batangas. The Find a Grave index gives his death as 22 February 1959 and his burial in Bacolod, Negros Occidental. The NUMIDENT names his parents as Doroteo Lontok and Maria Barbaera (as transcribed). No obituary or newspaper notice was found. Spouse and children are not documented in any public record found. One user-submitted family tree snippet points to a spouse with the surname Odjar.

### Cited Findings
- The Find a Grave global index shows "Gregorio B. Lontok | 24 Dec 1894 | 22 Feb 1959 | Bacolod, Negros Occidental Province, Western Visayas, Philippines." (a) Source: [Ancestry, Global Find a Grave index, search "lontok"](https://www.ancestry.co.uk/search/collections/60541?name=_lontok)
- The NUMIDENT transcription reads: "LONTOK, GREGORIO B was born 24 December 1894 in BATANGAS BAT, son of DOROTEO LONTOK (father) and MARIA BARBAERA, was assigned Social Security number 576-70-0237, and is in the NUMIDENT public file of deceased persons. The Social Security field office at VARO APO928 SANFRAN CA received the claim 14 September 1970." (a for birth date and parents' names as indexed) Source: [SortedByName, Lontok](https://sortedbyname.com/letter_l/lontok.html)
- An Ancestry family-tree search snippet for the surname Odjar shows a person whose "Spouse, Gregorio B Lontok (1894-xxxx)." This is user-submitted and the full tree was not viewed. (c, unverified tree) Source: [Ancestry family trees, "Odjar"](https://www.ancestry.com.au/search/categories/42?name=_Odjar)
- Claim check. "Born c. 1894-1896" is (a), narrowed to 24 Dec 1894 by two indexes. Death on 22 Feb 1959 with burial in Bacolod is (a), from the Find a Grave index only. The parents are (a) as NUMIDENT-indexed, with "Barbaera" possibly a transcription error. The spouse is (c) and appears only in an unverified tree snippet. Children are not documented in any public source found.

### Inferences
- "VARO APO928" probably refers to a U.S. Veterans Administration regional office. APO 928 appears to be a San Francisco military postal routing used for the Philippines. A 1970 claim through that office suggests survivors filed a VA-related claim, possibly tied to wartime service or a dependency benefit. This is an inference from the office code only. No service record was found.
- A death and burial in Bacolod in 1959 fits a man who stayed in Negros Occidental after the war.

### Gaps
- The Find a Grave memorial page itself (with photos, cemetery name, or linked family) could not be opened. Only the Ancestry index row was seen.
- FamilySearch (civil registration, Catholic records, and Philippine death records for Negros Occidental in 1959) requires login and was not searched.
- No obituary, newspaper mention, or veterans record (PVAO guerrilla rosters, U.S. NARA Philippine Archives Collection) was found for him.
- Taysan or Rosario parish records for an 1894 baptism were not searched.

### Inferences
- Under Filipino naming custom, a child's middle name is usually the mother's maiden surname. A child of Gregorio Lontok and an Odejar mother would therefore carry the middle initial "O." This is only a convention. No source seen ties any "O." Lontok to Gregorio or to an Odejar mother.
- "Odjar" in the Ancestry tree may be a variant or mistranscription of "Odejar," as the user suggests. No primary record was found to settle the spelling.
